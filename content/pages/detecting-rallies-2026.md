⚠️ International Legal Notice

<strong>Your Responsibility:</strong> Metal detecting laws vary significantly across Europe. Ignorance of the law is not a defence. You must ensure you have valid permissions and insurance (such as NCMD) before travelling.

### Quick Guide to European Restrictions

FRANCE <strong>Strictly Regulated.</strong> Illegal to search for history/art without a Prefectural license (rarely granted). "Leisure" detecting is a legal grey area and risky.

IRELAND <strong>Illegal.</strong> Strictly prohibited to search for archaeological objects anywhere. You cannot use a detector near monuments without a state license.

SPAIN <strong>Strict.</strong> generally prohibited on any historical sites. Laws vary by region (Andalusia is very strict), often requiring licenses that are hard for tourists to get.

ITALY <strong>Mixed.</strong> Beaches are generally tolerated. Inland searching for antiquities is strictly forbidden; all finds belong to the state.

BELGIUM <strong>License Required.</strong> In Flanders, you must have a specific metal detecting license from the agency for Immovable Heritage.

NETHERLANDS <strong>Allowed (With Rules).</strong> Generally permitted if you do not dig deeper than 30cm and stay away from registered monuments.

<em>\*This information is for guidance only and laws may change. Always check with local authorities.</em>
