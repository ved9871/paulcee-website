### Quick Guide to European Restrictions

## What Are the Metal Detecting Laws in Europe for UK Travellers?

Metal detecting laws vary drastically across Europe, and UK detectorists cannot assume UK-style rules apply abroad. In countries like Ireland and France, detecting without a hard-to-get state license is illegal, whereas the Netherlands allows shallow detecting with landowner permission.

---

### Is metal detecting legal in France?

- No, not without a permit. Under the French Heritage Code, it is illegal to use a metal detector to search for objects of historical, prehistoric, or archaeological interest without authorization from the Regional Prefect—which is rarely granted to hobbyists.

---

### Can you take a metal detector to Ireland?

- No. Under the National Monuments Act, it is strictly illegal to use a metal detector to search for archaeological objects anywhere in the Republic of Ireland, or to possess one near a monument without prior written consent from the Minister.

---

### Are you allowed to metal detect in Spain?

- In most regions, no. Metal detecting on historical or archaeological sites is prohibited across Spain, and autonomous regions like Andalusia enforce strict licensing laws that are rarely issued to visiting tourists.

---

### Is metal detecting allowed on beaches in Italy?

- Beach detecting for modern coins and jewellery is generally tolerated along most Italian coastlines, provided you check local council (\*Comune\*) seasonal rules. Searching inland for antiquities is strictly illegal, as all archaeological finds belong to the Italian state.

---

### Do you need a license to metal detect in Belgium?

- Yes. In Flanders, anyone aged 18 or over must apply for a recognized metal detectorist license through the Flanders Heritage Agency (\*Onroerend Erfgoed\*), dig no deeper than 30 cm, and report archaeological finds.

---

### What are the metal detecting rules in the Netherlands?

- Metal detecting is legal in the Netherlands on private land with the owner's permission, provided you dig no deeper than 30 cm, avoid registered archaeological monuments, and report significant finds to the Portable Antiquities of the Netherlands (PAN).

> Disclaimer: This guide is for general information only and national or regional laws can change without notice. Always verify current local legislation and secure valid travel/detecting insurance before taking a detector abroad.
