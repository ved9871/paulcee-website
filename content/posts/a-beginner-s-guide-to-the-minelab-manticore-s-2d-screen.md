### The Problem with "Old School" Numbers

To understand why the Manticore is special, you have to understand the limitation of traditional detectors.

Most detectors operate on a 1D (One-Dimensional) scale. They give you a single number (e.g., "55") based on conductivity.

<strong>The Issue:</strong> A gold ring and a modern aluminium pull tab often share the exact same conductivity. A detector relying only on ID numbers can't tell the difference. You have to dig it to find out.

### Enter the 2D Map: Seeing the Shape of Sound

The Minelab Manticore doesn't just measure conductivity; it measures ferrous limits (magnetic properties) at the same time. It plots this information on a visual graph.

Here is the simple breakdown of the screen layout:

![how to use the minelab manticore 2D screen in real world metal detecting](/img/2d-blog.webp)

<strong>The Horizontal Line (Centre):</strong> This is the "Non-Ferrous" zone. Treasure lives here. Coins, silver, and copper usually appear as tight dots directly on or near this centre line.

<strong>The Vertical Space (Top &amp; Bottom):</strong> This is the "Ferrous" zone. Iron junk lives here. Nails, wire, and rusty bits usually appear scattered at the very top or very bottom of the screen.

### Real-World Scenarios: What to Look For

Here is how to interpret the shapes ("traces") you see on the screen to decide if you should dig.

### 1. The Solid Dot (Dig It!)

<strong>The Target:</strong> A silver coin or copper penny (Modern Copper coins do have an "iron Core" and will sometimes give a tone as-well as going into the upper ferrous limits.

<strong>The Screen:</strong> You will see a tight, round dot sitting perfectly on the centre horizontal line. It doesn't move much as you swing back and forth. This is a clean, conductive target.

### 2. The "Smear" (Caution!)

<strong>The Target:</strong> A jagged piece of aluminium can slaw or an oddly shaped lead object. The Screen: Instead of a tight dot, the graphic looks like a smear or a blob that stretches diagonally. While it might sound good, the visual shape tells you the object is irregular.

### 3. The Bottle Cap Trick (The Money Saver)

<strong>The Target:</strong> A steel bottle cap. The Screen: This is where the Manticore shines. On a normal detector, this sounds like a coin. On the Manticore, the target might start near the centre line, but as you swing, the visual trace will drag or "hook" down into the ferrous (iron) zone at the bottom.

<strong>The Verdict:</strong> If the dot drags into the iron zone, walk away.

### Rapid-Fire Tips for Manticore Beginners

<strong>Trust Your Ears First:</strong> The Manticore’s audio is incredibly descriptive. Use the screen to confirm what your ears are telling you, not to replace them.
