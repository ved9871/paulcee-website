Have you ever wondered what is lying just a few inches beneath your boots? From centuries-old hammered silver and Roman bronze coins to everyday buttons and buckles that tell a story, metal detecting gives you a direct, hands-on connection to Britain’s past. 

Whether you have been thinking about trying the hobby for months, looking for an excuse to get out into the fresh air, or dropping hints for your first detector this Christmas, getting started is far simpler—and more affordable—than most people realise. Having spent years testing machines in the field as an official **_Minelab Detexpert_**, I always tell newcomers the same thing: you do not need to buy the most expensive flagship detector on day one, nor do you need years of experience to make great finds. Here is everything you need to know to get off on the right foot.

## More Than Just Chasing Old Coins 

Britain holds thousands of years of layered history—from Iron Age and Roman settlements to Saxon, medieval, and Victorian communities. People have been dropping coins, tools, and personal items across our fields and beaches for millennia, and plenty of it is still waiting to be found. Of course, not every beep under your coil will be a museum piece. You will dig plenty of rusty nails, ring pulls, and scrap lead along the way. Every seasoned detectorist does—it is simply part of clearing the ground to unmask the good targets hiding beneath. 

Beyond the finds pouch, detecting gets you walking miles outdoors, exploring new countryside, and researching the local history right on your doorstep.

## Where Can You Actually Go Detecting in the UK? 

Before heading out, make sure you know where you stand legally—you cannot simply walk onto public parks, common land, or historic sites and start digging.

* **Private Farmland & Pasture**: You must always obtain [**_Explicit permission from the landowner_**](https://www.paulcee.co.uk/blog/how-to-get-permission-to-metal-detect/) first (ideally in writing). Always check that the land is not a Scheduled Monument or Site of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI).

## What Can You Realistically Expect to Find? 

Social Media highlight posts make it look like every field is carpeted in hammered Coins and Gold, but real world detecting is a game of patience. On an average detecting day, you might come home with a Few crusty Georgian or Victorian copper coins, a button or a (few hundred), a musket ball, a bit of buckle fragment, and a pouch full of scrap. 

Yet pulling your first 500 year old coin or complete decorated button out of the ground gives a sense of satisfaction that never gets old. Always record where you find historical items, and remember that under the **Treasure Act 1996** in England, Wales, and Northern Ireland, any find that potentially qualifies as Treasure must be reported to your local **Finds Liaison Officer (FLO)** within **14 days of finding out it was a treasure find**.

##  Don't Over-Complicate Your First Outings 

The biggest mistake beginners make is constantly tweaking settings every ten minutes before they have learned what their machine is telling them. 

Keep your first few sessions simple: 

- Pick one factory mode (**Park,** **Field or Beach**). These are always the best until you are familiar with your detector settings and what they do.
- Set your sensitivity to a stable, quiet level where the machine isn't chattering falsely. 
- Spend time listening to how different metals sound, and sweep low and slow with overlapping passes.
- Test a few coins, a gold ring, and some coins of various types next to a rusty nail on top of the grass at home so your ears learn the difference between a crisp, repeatable tone and an erratic/broken and iron tone.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) 

### How much should a beginner spend on a first metal detector? 

You can get started comfortably between **£200 and £450** with a modern, waterproof multi-frequency detector like the [**_Minelab Vanquish 360_** ](https://www.paulcee.co.uk/minelab-vanquish-60-series/)or [**_X-Terra Elite_**](https://www.paulcee.co.uk/minelab-x-terra/), leaving room in your [budget for a pinpointer](https://www.paulcee.co.uk/minelab-pro-find/), sturdy [**_digging spade_**](https://www.paulcee.co.uk/detecting-accessories/), and [**_finds pouch._**](https://www.paulcee.co.uk/detecting-accessories/) 

### Do I need a licence to go metal detecting in the UK? 

No, you do not need a government licence to own or use a metal detector in the UK. However, you **_MUST_** have the landowner's permission before detecting on any private land, and you must strictly avoid [Scheduled Monuments and protected archaeological sites](https://www.paulcee.co.uk/old-maps-for-metal-detecting/). Some Beaches however metal detecting is not permitted without a permit or prior approval. [**_Check out the Crown Permits._**](https://www.paulcee.co.uk/links/) It is also a good idea to have [Public Liability Insurance](https://www.paulcee.co.uk/links/) in place, usually the [NCMD](https://www.paulcee.co.uk/links/) or [AMDS](https://www.amds.org.uk/)

### What is the best metal detector for both fields and beaches? 

A simultaneous multi-frequency detector such as the **Minelab Vanquish 360** is ideal for beginners who want to search both fields and wet beach sand, as Multi-IQ handles conductive saltwater minerals far better than traditional single-frequency starter machines. {{youtube id="TctUfPNIq4I" title="Minelab Vanquish Settings and search Profiles Explained"}}

### Is a pinpointer really necessary for beginners?

 While you can detect without one, a handheld pinpointer is easily the best accessory a newcomer can buy. It locates small coins in muddy clods within seconds, cuts down digging time, and reduces the risk of damaging fragile finds with your spade.
